### 02.8 — Bilingual copy scaffolding (ES/EN)

**What & why.** The experience ships in **Spanish and English**. On a normal site that's a late,
mechanical concern; here it is structural, because text is *choreographed* — words animate in, hold for
a beat, and move out on timings tuned to their length. Spanish runs materially longer than English for
the same message, so a layout and a motion system built against one language will break when the other
arrives: lines wrap where they shouldn't, and timings tuned to a short English phrase feel rushed on its
Spanish equivalent.

This story therefore belongs in *this* epic rather than Epic 03. It builds the scaffolding and proves
both languages against the real choreography — the **final** copy is written later, with the brand voice,
in Epic 03. What's being de-risked here is the structure, not the wording.

**Value archetype.** Enabler (the scaffolding every later string passes through) + Acquisition (a
visitor reading the gate instructions in their own language is more likely to finish).

**Surface / UX fit.** Cross-cutting across all four states. Establishes the language-selection convention
recorded as [PDR-004](../../product/decisions.md), alongside the product's canonical vocabulary.

**Prioritization.** Runs alongside the scene stories rather than after them — retrofitting a second
language into finished motion timing is the expensive path, and avoiding that is the whole reason this
story sits in Epic 02.

**Edge cases — resolved.**
- **Which language a visitor gets.** Detected from the browser, defaulting to Spanish, with a manual
  override available — a visitor must never be trapped in a language they don't read.
- **Where the switch lives.** It cannot become a navigation element competing with the experience; it is
  present but subordinate ([PDR-004](../../product/decisions.md)).
- **Switching mid-run.** Changes the language without restarting the experience or losing form input.
- **Text length variance.** Layouts are validated against the longer language, and motion timings must
  hold for both — this is the specific failure this story exists to prevent.
- **Validation and error messages.** Localized too, including 02.5's inline errors. A Spanish visitor
  hitting an English error is a visible seam at the most sensitive moment in the flow.
- **Placeholder copy.** Written as real, plausible sentences of realistic length in both languages — not
  lorem ipsum, which would hide exactly the length and wrapping problems this story is meant to surface.

**Edge cases — deferred.**
- Considered, deferred — a third language. Nothing suggests one is needed; the scaffolding shouldn't be
  over-generalized for a hypothetical.
- Considered, deferred — locale-aware phone or date formatting beyond what 02.5 already needs.

**Acceptance criteria (product-level).**
- Every visitor-facing string in all four scenes exists in both Spanish and English.
- Language is detected from the browser, defaults to Spanish, and can be overridden manually.
- Switching language mid-run preserves position in the flow and anything typed.
- No scene breaks its layout or its motion timing in either language.
- Validation and error messages are localized.
- Placeholder copy is realistic in length in both languages.
